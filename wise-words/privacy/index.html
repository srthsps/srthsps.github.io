Privacy Policy

Wise Words · Effective date: 1 November 2026

Wise Words ("the game") is made by Sarath PS ("I", "me"). This policy explains what information the game collects, why, and what you can do about it. If you have any questions, email me at srthsps22@gmail.com.

1. The short version

  • The game offers optional purchases through Google Play (coin packs and a Supporter pack). Google handles the payment: I never see your card or payment details (section 6).
  • It uses Google Firebase to collect anonymous gameplay statistics and crash reports, so I can fix bugs and improve the game (section 7). These never include your nickname, and they are switched off in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland.
  • It has video ads: optional ones you choose to watch for a reward (a free hint or extra coins), and, later in the climb, an occasional full-screen ad in the pause between two ledges, never during a puzzle. The Supporter pack turns those off. Ads are provided by Google AdMob and its partner ad networks, which use device information as described in section 5.
  • It never asks for your real name, email address, phone number, photos, contacts or location.
  • To show the world ranking, it stores the nickname and animal avatar you choose, a random anonymous account ID, and your game scores.
  • So your progress isn't lost with your phone, it keeps a backup copy of your game progress on the same server, linked only to that anonymous account ID (section 3).
  • If you play with friends in a room, the other players in that room see your nickname, buddy, score and the words you try (section 3).
  • During testing builds only, it also sends basic device information so I can see which test devices are working.
  • You can delete your data at any time, right in the game (see section 10).

2. Information saved on your device

The game keeps a save file on your phone with your progress: your current ledge, coins, the words in your Word Book, treasure words found, your Daily Bloom calendar and streak, daily gifts collected, the looks and prizes you own, a record of purchases already delivered (Google Play purchase tokens, so nothing is delivered twice), sound and vibration settings, your nickname and avatar, and a sign-in token for the ranking. It stays on your device. If your phone's Google backup is turned on, Android may include this file in your phone's backup, so your progress can come back after reinstalling or on a new phone; this backup is handled by Google under your Google account's settings. When you uninstall, Android may offer to keep the game's data; otherwise the file is deleted.

If you turn on Daily Bloom reminders, the game schedules them on your phone. To pick a good time, it remembers the hours you usually open the game, on your device only. Nothing about reminders is sent anywhere, and you can turn them off at any time in the game or in your phone's settings.

When you share a room code or a result, the game opens your phone's share menu with the text; you choose where it goes (for example a chat app), and the game doesn't receive or keep what you do there.

3. Information sent to the ranking server

When you are online, the game takes part in the world ranking. For this it sends:

  • Your nickname (2 to 12 characters, chosen by you) and the animal avatar you picked. Nicknames are checked against a list of rude words.
  • A random anonymous account ID that is created for your installation. It is not linked to your Google account, email or phone number.
  • Your results for each finished ledge: the ledge number, the number of wrong words, hints used and bonus words found. The server works out your points from these.
  • The times your results were submitted, so the server can spot cheating and keep the ranking fair.
  • A backup copy of your game progress: the same things as the save file in section 2 (ledge, coins, Word Book words, Daily Bloom calendar, looks and prizes you own, the record of delivered purchases, settings, nickname and avatar), but never the sign-in token. It lets your progress be restored if your phone is lost or the game is reinstalled. Only your installation can update it, and other players can never see it.

Other players can see your nickname, avatar, points and highest ledge on the ranking. They cannot see anything else about you.

Playing with friends: when you make or join a room with its code, the server stores that you are in the room, your seat, the words you find there and your score in the room. The other players in the same room (at most six, who joined with the same code) see your nickname, buddy and score, and every word you try, live while you play. The words you try are passed on to them straight away and are not stored. Only the room's players can see a room. A room and everything in it are deleted when everyone has left it, or after two hours without play. There is no chat.

Please don't use your real full name or other personal details as your nickname.

The game works offline. If you are not connected, your results wait on your device and are sent the next time you are online.

4. Information sent in testing builds

Builds of the game handed out for testing (for example, a Google Play closed test) include a small testing tool called Tester Pulse. It tells me which test devices installed the game and when it was last opened. When the game opens, and then about every 30 minutes while it is in use, it sends:

  • a random install ID created for the installation;
  • the device manufacturer and model, Android version, device type (phone or tablet) and language setting;
  • the game's version and package name;
  • an app set ID provided by Google Play services, which identifies the device to apps from the same developer and is not used for advertising;
  • the times the game was opened;
  • if you joined the test through a tester invitation link, the code from that link, so your device can be matched to your tester entry.

This information is only used to manage testing. It is not sent by builds that don't include the testing tool.

5. Ads

Some screens offer a short video ad in exchange for a reward, for example "Watch an ad for a free hint" or "Double your coins". These only play when you tap the offer, and some are limited each day.

From ledge 15 of the climb, the game may also show an occasional full-screen ad in the short pause between two ledges. It never appears during a puzzle, the Daily Bloom or a friends' room; there is at most one every few ledges, several minutes apart, and only a limited number a day. Players who bought the Supporter pack never see these. The game shows no banner ads.

These ads are provided by Google AdMob. AdMob may let other ad networks compete to show an ad ("mediation"); the networks the game works with are AppLovin and Unity Ads. To show an ad, count it and prevent fraud, Google and the network showing the ad may collect:

  • your device's advertising ID (or the app set ID if the advertising ID isn't available);
  • your IP address, which can indicate your approximate location;
  • device information such as model, operating system and language;
  • how you interact with the ad (for example, whether it was watched to the end).

The game asks AdMob for ads suitable for all ages only. In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form asks whether ads may be personalised before any ad is loaded. Where the law gives you privacy choices (including the EEA, the UK, Switzerland and some US states), a "Privacy choices" link on your profile screen lets you change or withdraw them at any time.

You can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your phone's settings (Settings > Google > Ads, or Settings > Privacy > Ads). Their privacy policies explain how they use this information: Google https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites, AppLovin https://www.applovin.com/privacy/ and Unity https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy

I do not receive your advertising ID or any personal information from the ads.

6. In-app purchases

The game offers optional purchases through Google Play: packs of coins, and a Supporter pack (coins, a Supporter frame for your buddy, and no full-screen ads between ledges). Nothing needs to be bought to play or to do well: coins are also earned by playing, and ledge prizes can only be won by climbing.

Payments are made in Google Play, under Google Play's terms and Google's privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy). I never receive your card or other payment details. When you buy something, Google Play tells the game what was bought and gives it a purchase token; the game uses it only to deliver your purchase, once, and keeps it in your save file and its backup copy (sections 2 and 3). Google Play gives me sales reports with information such as the order number, the item, the price, the date and the country, which I use for accounting, refunds and support.

Refunds are handled by Google Play. Parents can make Google Play ask for a password or fingerprint before any purchase (in the Play Store app: Settings > Authentication).

7. Analytics and crash reports

To find bugs and see which parts of the game are too hard, too easy or confusing, the game uses Google Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics. They receive:

  • events about how the game is played, for example: which ledge you started, finished or left and how long it took, how many hints and wrong words it needed, Daily Bloom results, daily gifts collected, items bought with coins, whether an optional ad was offered and watched, when a full-screen ad was shown, which pack a purchase was for (never payment details), and when the share menu was opened;
  • if the game crashes: a crash report with the device model, Android version, game version and what the game was doing at the time;
  • a random Firebase installation ID created for this installation, basic device information (model, Android version, language), an approximate location (country or region) worked out from your IP address, and when the game was used.

These reports never include your nickname, avatar, your ranking account or anything you type. The game tells Firebase not to read your device's advertising ID, and this information is not used for advertising.

In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, analytics and crash reports are switched off. Elsewhere they start only after Google's consent check confirms that no consent is required in your region.

Analytics event data is kept for about two months and crash reports for 90 days; summary statistics without any installation ID may be kept longer. Because these reports are anonymous and not linked to your nickname, they can't be looked up or deleted for one player. Uninstalling the game or clearing its data creates a new installation ID.

8. Why the information is used

Your information is used only to:

  • run the world ranking and show your place on it;
  • keep the ranking fair (for example, limiting how fast results can be submitted);
  • save your progress;
  • during testing, see which test devices are working and fix problems;
  • show the ads described in section 5;
  • deliver the purchases you make (section 6);
  • find bugs and improve the game, using the statistics and crash reports described in section 7.

I never sell or rent your information. The ranking and testing information is not used for advertising.

9. Who processes the information

  • Supabase stores the ranking data (nicknames, avatars, anonymous account IDs and scores) and the backup copies of game progress. The servers are in the Mumbai, India region.
  • Cloudflare hosts the testing service that receives the Tester Pulse information.
  • Google Play services provides the app set ID on testing builds.
  • Google AdMob provides the ads and receives the information listed in section 5; AppLovin and Unity Ads may show ads through AdMob and receive the same kind of information.
  • Google Play handles purchases and payments (section 6).
  • Google Firebase (Analytics and Crashlytics) receives the statistics and crash reports described in section 7, and processes them on my behalf.

All information is sent over encrypted connections (HTTPS). Supabase and Cloudflare process the data on my behalf and only to provide their services. Google, AppLovin and Unity handle ad data, and Google handles payments, under their own privacy policies.

10. Keeping and deleting your information

Ranking information and the backup copy of your progress are kept while you play, so your place on the ranking and your progress are kept. Room information is deleted when the room closes (see section 3). Testing information is kept while the test runs and for a reasonable time afterwards.

To delete your data in the game: tap your profile card at the top of the main menu, then tap "Delete my data" and confirm. This immediately deletes your nickname, avatar, anonymous account, scores, the backup copy of your progress and your place in any room from the server, and resets the game on your phone. You need an internet connection for this.

You can also email srthsps22@gmail.com with your nickname to ask me to delete it. Approximate points and the date you started playing help me find the right entry. I will delete it within 30 days and let you know when it's done. Uninstalling the game deletes everything stored on your device, but not your ranking data, so use "Delete my data" first if you want that gone too.

You can also email me to ask what information I hold about you, or to correct it.

11. Children

Wise Words is made for kids and grown-ups. It does not ask for any personal information beyond a nickname of your choice, and it has no chat or messages between players. Only ads suitable for all ages are requested: reward videos play only when a player chooses one, and the occasional full-screen ad between ledges only appears from ledge 15. Purchases go through Google Play, where parents can require a password for every purchase. If you are a parent and believe your child has sent information you want removed, email me and I will delete it.

12. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be published in the same place with a new effective date. If a change matters for how your information is used, the game or its store listing will say so.
